# Tissue signals imprint ILC2 identity with anticipatory function

- Verified title: Tissue signals imprint ILC2 identity with anticipatory function
- Authors: Roberto R. Ricardo-Gonzalez, Steven J. Van Dyken, Christoph Schneider, Jinwoo Lee, Jesse C. Nussbaum, Hong-Erh Liang, Dedeepya Vaka, Walter L. Eckalbar, Ari B. Molofsky, David J. Erle, and Richard M. Locksley

- Volume/pages: 19, 1093-1099
- DOI: 10.1038/s41590-018-0201-4

## Source Type

- primary tissue-imprinting ILC2 study
- main cell focus: ILC2
- main compartments: lung, gut/small intestinal lamina propria, skin, fat, and bone marrow
- main experimental system: mouse cytokine-reporter and cytokine-receptor-deficient models with bulk RNA-seq and scRNA-seq

## Evidence Profile

- Overall confidence: high for mouse tissue-specific ILC2 transcriptional organization and receptor-output specialization.
- Species: mouse experimental evidence. Human ILC heterogeneity is discussed as prior context, but this source should not be cited as a direct human comparative dataset.
- Perturbations: germ-free comparison, combined TSLPR / IL-33R / IL-25 pathway deficiency, IL-18 deficiency, IL-18 plus TSLP stimulation, and IL-18/ST2-linked analyses.
- Assays: reporter flow cytometry, cell sorting, cytokine assays, bulk RNA-seq, scRNA-seq of 35,396 sorted ILC2s, and tissue challenge experiments.

## Why It Matters Here

This source supports a core ILC2 interpretation rule: tissue labels are not cosmetic. Lung, gut, skin, fat, and bone marrow ILC2s carry different receptor and transcriptional programs even at rest, so lung ILC2 mechanisms should not be automatically generalized from gut or skin studies, and extrapulmonary mechanisms should remain tissue-labeled.

## Key Findings

- Resting tissue ILC2s showed basal activation and tissue-dependent transcriptional programs across lung, gut, fat, skin, and bone marrow.
- Bulk RNA-seq and scRNA-seq grouped ILC2s by tissue of origin, supporting tissue-organized ILC2 identity rather than a single uniform ILC2 state.
- Germ-free mice did not show major loss of the reported tissue ILC2 activation program, arguing that commensal microbiota were not the main driver of these steady-state tissue programs.
- Combined TSLPR, IL-33R, and IL-25 pathway deficiency altered homeostatic IL-5 reporter output in a tissue-dependent manner, especially in lung, fat, and gut, but did not erase the broader tissue-specific ILC2 transcriptomic structure.
- Receptor programs were tissue biased: Il1rl1 / ST2 was enriched in fat and lung ILC2s, Il17rb / IL-25R in gut ILC2s, and Il18r1 / IL-18R1 in skin ILC2s and smaller lung and bone marrow subsets.
- IL-18 plus TSLP increased IL-13 output from sorted skin and lung ILC2s, and IL-18-linked perturbation impaired IL-18R-bearing ILC2 subsets in the reported systems.

## Claim-Level Confidence

- High confidence:
  mouse ILC2 identity and receptor programs are strongly tissue organized in the reported steady-state systems.
- High confidence:
  lung ILC2s are not interchangeable with gut, skin, fat, or bone marrow ILC2s for mechanistic inference.
- Medium-high confidence:
  epithelial cytokine pathways such as IL-33, IL-25, and TSLP shape tissue-specific homeostatic output, but they do not fully define tissue identity.
- Medium confidence:
  IL-18 can activate IL-18R-bearing ILC2 subsets, including lung subsets, but disease-specific pulmonary relevance requires separate disease-context sources.
- Low confidence:
  this source alone does not establish asthma mechanism, human lung causality, or clinical therapeutic response.

## Methods and Context

- The study used 7- to 14-week-old mice with cytokine reporter alleles, including Arg1/Yarg, IL-5/Red5, and IL-13/Smart13 reporters.
- Tissues included lung, small intestinal lamina propria, skin, perigonadal adipose tissue, and bone marrow.
- The combined cytokine-pathway-deficient system lacked responsiveness to TSLP, IL-33, and IL-25 signaling.
- RNA-seq and scRNA-seq data generated in the study were deposited under GEO accession GSE117568.

## Caveats

- The article is best used as tissue-imprinting and receptor-specialization evidence, not as direct disease-causality evidence.
- The human relevance is interpretive and background-linked in this source page unless another source supplies direct human tissue data.
- Do not merge the IL-18 tissue-imprinting branch with severe-asthma sputum IL-1beta/IL-18 plasticity unless both sources are cited and compartment labels are explicit.
