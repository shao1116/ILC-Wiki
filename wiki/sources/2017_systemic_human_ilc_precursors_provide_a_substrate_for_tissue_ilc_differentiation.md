# Systemic Human ILC Precursors Provide a Substrate for Tissue ILC Differentiation

## Citation

- Verified title: Systemic Human ILC Precursors Provide a Substrate for Tissue ILC Differentiation
- Authors: Ai Ing Lim, Yan Li, Silvia Lopez-Lastra, Ralph Stadhouders, Franziska Paul, Mohamed Azami, Marjolein A. B. Roels, Nuria V. O. Gomes, Mohamed Kandalla, Alexandra Cupedo, Hans Yssel, James P. Di Santo, and colleagues
- Journal/year: Cell 168, 1086-1100.e10 (2017)
- DOI: 10.1016/j.cell.2017.02.021
- Data resources noted in the PDF: GSE90834 and GSE90640.

## Ingest Mode

- Mode: `source-reviewed evidence note`
- Review depth: source text, main figures/results logic, tissue compartments, clonal assays, humanized-mouse transfer, and RORC-deficiency boundary were checked before integration into topic/entity/digest pages.
- Reusable boundary: this source supports human ILCP identity, tissue distribution, and differentiation potential, including adult lung ILCP potential. It should not be used as a direct pulmonary-disease mechanism.

## Source Type

- Human primary developmental and precursor-biology study with in vitro clonal assays, transcriptomic/epigenomic profiling, human tissue analysis, and humanized-mouse engraftment.
- Main biological focus: circulating and tissue human innate lymphoid cell precursors (ILCPs), not mature effector ILC3 disease function.

## Evidence Profile

- Species: human primary samples; humanized BRGS mouse transfer system.
- Tissue/compartment: peripheral blood, fetal liver, cord blood, adult lung, gut, tonsil, spleen/lung/gut/liver compartments after humanized-mouse transfer.
- Disease/model anchor: developmental/precursor biology; RORC-deficient patient context for lineage-potential boundary. No primary lung-disease model.
- Perturbations and assays: fluorescence sorting of CD117+ ILC-lineage populations, OP9 and OP9-DL4 culture, cytokine-conditioned differentiation, clonal assays, RNA-seq, ChIP-seq/H3K4me2 epigenomic profiling, adoptive transfer into humanized BRGS mice, and analysis of RORC-deficient patients.

## Key Findings

- The paper identifies systemic and tissue human ILCPs that do not yet express mature ILC transcription-factor/cytokine programs but carry poised chromatin at mature ILC signature loci.
- Peripheral-blood CD117+ ILC-lineage cells are not equivalent to mature gut ILC3s. They lack NKp44 and RORgammat protein and do not produce IL-17A or IL-22 ex vivo, but they express precursor-associated features such as CD45RA and IL1R1.
- Human ILCPs can generate multiple ILC lineages in culture and after humanized-mouse transfer, including EOMES+ NK cells, IFN-gamma+ ILC1s, IL-13+ ILC2s, and IL-22+ or IL-17A+ ILC3 outputs under permissive conditions.
- Adult human lung contains CD117+ ILC-lineage populations that include NKp44+ and RORgammat+ ILC3-like cells as well as an NKp44- fraction with ILCP activity. The lung finding supports local precursor potential, not disease causality.
- RORC-deficient patient ILCPs were present and retained potential for NK, ILC1, ILC2, and IL-22+ ILC3 outputs, but failed to generate IL-17A+ ILC3s. This supports a narrow RORC requirement for IL-17A+ ILC3 generation rather than a global absence of ILCPs.
- Notch-ligand context and cytokines shape differentiation output. OP9-DL4 and cytokine conditions such as IL-2, IL-7, IL-1beta, IL-23, and IL-12 help reveal lineage potential in vitro.

## Claim-Level Confidence

- High confidence: human CD117+ ILCPs are a developmental precursor compartment distinct from mature effector ILC subsets.
- High confidence: human ILCPs can generate NK, ILC1, ILC2, and ILC3 outputs under the source-specific assay conditions.
- High confidence: adult human lung contains CD117+ ILC-lineage populations with ILCP activity and mature ILC3-like fractions, supporting a human lung precursor/tissue-differentiation boundary.
- Medium-high confidence: RORC is required for IL-17A+ ILC3 differentiation in this source's patient and culture context, while IL-22+ ILC3 potential and other ILC outputs can remain.
- Low confidence: any claim that this paper demonstrates a pulmonary disease mechanism, airway inflammation pathway, or disease-specific mature ILC3 effector function.

## Methods and Context

- The strongest evidence comes from combined phenotype, transcriptome, chromatin, clonal differentiation, patient-deficiency, and humanized-mouse transfer logic.
- Humanized BRGS transfer showed CD117+ ILCP-derived Lin-CD7+ human ILC-lineage cells in multiple organs, including lung, gut, liver, and spleen. This supports in vivo differentiation potential, but the recipient system is not a human lung-disease model.
- Adult lung observations should be cited as human lung precursor/tissue potential and taxonomy context, not as asthma/COPD/ARDS causality.

## Caveats

- Do not treat all CD117+ human ILC-lineage cells as mature ILC3s. The paper explicitly separates peripheral-blood ILCPs from mature gut CD117+ ILC3s.
- Do not use ILCP differentiation potential as evidence that a mature ILC3 state is causing a pulmonary disease phenotype.
- Keep RORC-deficiency conclusions narrow: the source supports loss of IL-17A+ ILC3 generation while other ILC outputs remain possible.

## Contradiction and Supersession

- This source is a taxonomy guardrail for human ILC work. It helps separate precursor potential, mature ILC state, tissue residency, and disease-associated effector function.
- It complements human lung ILC subset papers by adding precursor biology, but it does not supersede lung-disease-specific studies.
