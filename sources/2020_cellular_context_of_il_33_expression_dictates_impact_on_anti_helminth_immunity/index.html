    Cellular context of IL-33 expression dictates impact on anti-helminth immunity

    Citation

    • Verified title: Cellular context of IL-33 expression dictates impact on anti-helminth immunity
    • Authors: Li-Yin Hung, Yukinori Tanaka, Karl Herbine, Christopher Pastore, Brenal Singh, Annabel Ferguson, Nisha Vora, Bonnie Douglas, Kelly Zullo, Edward M. Behrens, Tiffany Li Hui Tan, Michael A. Kohanski, Paul Bryce, Cailu Lin, Taku Kambayashi, Danielle R. Reed, Breann L. Brown, Noam A. Cohen, and De'Broski R. Herbert
    • Journal/year: Science Immunology 5, eabc6259 (2020)
    • DOI: 10.1126/sciimmunol.abc6259
    • Note: the PDF reports a correction published on 8 January 2021.

    Ingest Mode

    • Mode: source-reviewed evidence note
    • Review depth: source text, experimental context, main perturbations, and source-bounded conclusions were checked before integration into topic/entity/digest pages.
    • Reusable boundary: this source can support source-cell-specific IL-33 biology, epithelial IL-33 support of ILC2/type 2 anti-helminth immunity, and APC IL-33 support of ST2+GATA3+Foxp3+ Treg-mediated restraint in mouse intestinal helminth models. It should not be used as direct evidence for lung-disease causality.

    Source Type

    • Primary mechanistic immunology study.
    • Main model system: mouse gastrointestinal helminth infection and mucosal immune regulation.
    • Human context: sinonasal polyp and inferior-turbinate airway expression datasets used for conservation or localization context, not functional human causality.

    Evidence Profile

    • Species: mouse primary; human airway tissue/expression context.
    • Tissue/compartment: small-intestinal lamina propria, colon lamina propria, mesenteric lymph node, mouse lung immune-cell expression context, human sinonasal polyp and inferior turbinate.
    • Disease/model anchor: Nippostrongylus brasiliensis and Heligmosomoides polygyrus bakeri anti-helminth immunity; human CRSwNP expression context.
    • Perturbations and assays: VillinCreER Il33 deletion, CD11cCre Il33 deletion, Foxp3Cre Gata3 deletion, IL-2 immune complex, ST2+Foxp3+ Treg adoptive transfer, Mpeg1/perforin-2 perturbation, flow cytometry, immunofluorescence/confocal microscopy, qPCR/RNA-seq, helminth burden and egg-output measurements, cytokine assays, and DC-T cell coculture.

    Key Findings

    • IL-33 outcome depends strongly on the cellular source. Epithelial-cell IL-33 supported type 2 anti-helminth immunity, whereas CD11c+ APC-derived IL-33 supported a regulatory arm that restrained host-protective inflammation in the reported intestinal helminth systems.
    • In VillinCreER Il33-deficient mice, loss of intestinal epithelial IL-33 impaired N. brasiliensis clearance and reduced ILC2 frequencies in small-intestinal lamina propria and mesenteric lymph nodes, supporting an epithelial IL-33 -> ILC2/type 2 protection branch.
    • In CD11cCre Il33-deficient mice, loss of CD11c+ cell-derived IL-33 accelerated anti-helminth immunity in N. brasiliensis and H. polygyrus bakeri models and reduced intestinal ST2+GATA3+Foxp3+ Tregs, supporting an APC IL-33 -> Treg restraint branch.
    • Foxp3Cre Gata3 deletion, IL-2 immune complex treatment, and ST2+Foxp3+ Treg transfer experiments support the interpretation that ST2+GATA3+Foxp3+ Tregs can restrain anti-helminth type 2 immunity in this setting. Transfer of ST2- Tregs did not provide the same reversal in the reported experiment.
    • The study links DC-intrinsic perforin-2/Mpeg1 biology to IL-33 release and Treg expansion in vitro, adding a mechanism for how APC-derived IL-33 can reach the ST2+ Treg axis.
    • Mouse IL-33-GFP expression included myeloid APC populations in multiple tissues, including lung tissue, but the functional helminth/Treg conclusions are anchored mainly in intestinal and mesenteric-lymph-node experiments.
    • Human nasal-polyp and inferior-turbinate datasets support the presence of IL-33-associated APC/monocyte/macrophage/DC expression context in airway tissue, but they do not test whether the mouse intestinal Treg mechanism operates in human airway disease.

    Claim-Level Confidence

    • High confidence: in the reported mouse intestinal helminth models, epithelial IL-33 and CD11c+ APC IL-33 have divergent immunologic outputs.
    • High confidence: epithelial IL-33 supports ILC2-associated type 2 anti-helminth immunity in the source-specific mouse gut/MLN context.
    • High confidence: CD11c+ APC-derived IL-33 supports ST2+GATA3+Foxp3+ Treg maintenance/expansion and suppresses host-protective anti-helminth responses in the source-specific mouse gut/MLN context.
    • Medium-high confidence: Mpeg1/perforin-2 is part of the APC-intrinsic IL-33 release/Treg expansion mechanism in the experimental systems tested.
    • Medium confidence: human sinonasal data indicate airway expression/localization context for IL-33-associated APC biology.
    • Low confidence: direct extrapolation from this paper to pulmonary asthma, COPD, viral infection, or lung Treg mechanisms, unless a separate lung-focused source is cited.

    Methods and Context

    • The strongest causal evidence comes from conditional Il33 deletion in epithelial or CD11c-expressing cells, genetic Treg-context perturbation, IL-2 immune complex rescue logic, adoptive transfer of sorted ST2+ versus ST2- Foxp3+ Tregs, and helminth burden/egg-output outcomes.
    • N. brasiliensis has a lung-migration phase, but the durable immunologic conclusions used here are intestinal/MLN anti-helminth and Treg-regulation conclusions unless the source explicitly measured lung-specific outputs.
    • The human airway material is important for biological plausibility and conservation, but it is not a human perturbation experiment.

    Caveats

    • Do not write "IL-33 activates ILC2s" as a universal claim from this source. The paper shows that IL-33 source-cell context can change the direction and outcome of the immune response.
    • Do not promote APC-derived IL-33 -> ST2+GATA3+ Treg restraint to a direct lung-disease mechanism without matching pulmonary evidence.
    • Separate epithelial IL-33/ILC2 support from APC IL-33/Treg restraint; combining them into one undifferentiated IL-33 pathway would lose the main conclusion of the paper.

    Contradiction and Supersession

    • This source refines, rather than contradicts, the common ILC2 alarmin model. IL-33 can be a pro-type-2 epithelial alarmin in one cellular context and a Treg-supporting regulatory signal in another.
    • Lung ILC2 pages should keep this paper as a source-cell-context boundary, not as a replacement for direct lung IL-33/ST2 studies.
